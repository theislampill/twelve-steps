# Scope, sources and rights

## Scope and care

This project examines spiritual claims in selected AA literature. It distinguishes what
the books assert, what their arguments support, and what would require evidence about
a particular meeting or member. Narcotics Anonymous requires separate analysis of its
own sources. No endorsement is claimed from any cited organisation or author.

The essay argues that an offer of another
spiritual solution displaces the given prescription by the condition of its own
appeal to God’s direction. It openly adopts the Way of the Messengers, presented
through the Qur’an and Muhammad’s teaching, as the clear, authoritative and sufficient
religious answer. AA’s commitments about God’s will provide internal points of contact, but the complete argument is not presented
as wholly internal to AA. The companion chapter develops the same religious commitments.
Neither chapter assesses comparative treatment effectiveness, assigns hidden
motives, or treats membership or continuing struggle as proof of deficient faith.

This is not a treatment or withdrawal plan. Do not change medication or discontinue care
on its basis. Withdrawal after prolonged heavy drinking can be life-threatening and may
require medical help. The NIAAA guide describes professional treatment, medication and
mutual support options.[^care]

## Sources

The current AA citations use official English PDFs. The Big Book chapter files are from
the fourth edition; its official copyright leaf identifies that edition as 2001. The
date in a hosting URL is not an edition date. Chapters 1–2, 4–7 and 11 were consulted;
the argument chiefly uses Chapters 4–7 and 11. Exact passages and printed-to-PDF page
mappings are in the essay’s notes. Printed pp. 58–60, 83–84, 53 and 55 were also checked
as page images for v0.9.0; p. 74 was checked as text and image for v0.10.0.
The v0.10.0 source-role review also reread pp. 45–50, 93–94 and 164, and the 12&12’s
Step Three discussion on pp. 35 and 39–40. For v0.11.0 those structural passages
were reread, together with the progression on pp. 63, 72 and 75–76. The confession
sentence crossing pp. 74–75 was read in full, and p. 75 was checked as an image.
Numerical recovery claims and historical medical descriptions are not treated as
current clinical evidence.

The inspection of *Twelve Steps and Twelve Traditions* covers the complete commentaries
on Steps Two, Three and Eleven. Its official copyright leaf identifies original
publication in 1953 and reformatting/footnoting in 2021. The Conference-approved pamphlets
used are *Questions and Answers on Sponsorship* (P-15, revised 2025, printing code 2/26),
*Many Paths to Spirituality* (P-84, copyright 2014, printing code 10/25), and *The “God” Word*
(P-86, copyright 2018, printing code 8/25). Individual accounts within them remain
individual accounts. They are evidence of the range of experience published by AA, not
rules binding every group. For v0.12.0, the four expressions analysed in the essay
were checked in Big Book p. 59, 12&12 p. 26, P-15 pp. 13–14 and 20, P-84 p. 4, and
P-86 pp. 5–6. The spiritual/religious distinction is identified as a paraphrase of
the editorial explanation; attributed member speech is not silently made institutional
doctrine. Sources were checked on 2 October 2026; the record identifies
the actual PDF bytes. No survey of meetings was conducted.

The supplied Wordsmiths translation of Ibn Taymiyyah’s *Creed of Asfahani* (2024), the
al-Shatibi translation (2012), and El-Tobgui’s dissertation (2013) retain their separate
identities. The present use of *Asfahani* centres on printed pp. 16–17 and 36–37. Its
editorial subheadings are the translator’s. Al-Shatibi’s printed pp. 24–37 were read as
images, including pp. 31–32 on knowledge of benefits. The file has 641 PDF pages; its
publication page describes a 640-page book. An earlier claim that it was a 150-page
partial scan was an indexing error, corrected in v0.8.0.

The discussion of the *Darʾ* uses El-Tobgui’s secondary account, principally printed
pp. 160–174, 226–233 and 276–278. It is not presented as a fresh verification of the
Arabic original. Ibn al-Qayyim’s *al-Daʾ wa’l-Dawaʾ* is cited from the identified Arabic
edition and scan: edited by Muhammad Ajmal al-Islahi, fourth edition, 1440/2019.
Selected passages and their page images were checked; none of these books is represented
as having been read in full for this revision.

Qur’an passages are linked by surah and verse, hadith by collection and the cited
website’s numbering. For v0.11.0, Qur’an 5:3 and Muslim 1718a–b were checked in Arabic
and the displayed translations for the argument about sufficiency and religious
innovation. Grading is attributed where relevant. Al-Nawawi’s conditions of
repentance are identified as his chapter introduction, not mislabelled as a numbered
prophetic report. The five-fold account of *bidʿah*, the habit/worship distinction, and
the Maturidi–Ashʿari comparison use hosted primary Arabic passages. Their online
pagination or passage locators are stated; no different print edition is silently
substituted. The Arabic source discussion is paraphrase and analysis, not a new published
translation. Checking a web text is not an independent manuscript or transmission study.

The Millati Islami case uses the organisation’s published Steps, meeting format and
current meeting readings, including their appeal to Qur’an 16:97. For v0.11.0 the
readings’ statements about religious direction, integration, ultimate authority and
group purpose on PDF pp. 3–4 and 6 were also examined. Ibn Kathir’s
commentary on that verse is consulted in the identified English abridgement.
The two subsequent course scenarios are constructed comparisons, not reported practices.

For v0.12.0, Ibn al-Qayyim’s extended analysis of resemblance and wording in *Miftah
dar al-saʿadah* was checked in Arabic, continuous pp. 395–397 of the digital reproduction
identified in the essay. The host names Ibn Qaʾid’s third edition, 1440/2019; a physical
copy or title-page scan was not inspected. The analysis follows his report of Ibn
Taymiyyah’s sponge-and-glass counsel and is distinguished from that reported speech.
The essay paraphrases the checked Arabic; it does not quote an independently
verified English edition. Its application to AA belongs to this project.

[PROVENANCE.json](PROVENANCE.json) identifies the source PDFs, previous archive
identities and the scope of checking. Older entries retained from
the predecessor manifest are labelled as inherited where their original bytes were
not available for this revision. Hashes establish file identity, not the truth of a
source’s claims. The linked sources are not bundled with the archive.
