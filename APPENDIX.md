# Notes on grounds and evidence

Religious authority, the interpretation of an instruction and evidence of recovery
are distinct questions. These notes explain their scope in the [essay](THESIS.md).

## 1. What grounding establishes

An arrangement can be grounded in general religious instruction without reproducing
a revealed timetable. The instruction must authorise the religious ends and limits
the arrangement serves. The teacher’s choices remain choices; their usefulness does
not make each of them a condition God has prescribed for His help.

Agreement with particular teachings is therefore insufficient to establish the
authority of a whole spiritual programme. Honesty, prayer and repair can occur in
different accounts of the remedy. What needs grounds is the account actually offered,
including its specified conditions and assurances. Attaching a verse or using a
religious vocabulary does not supply those grounds for every added claim.

Genuine grounding removes the criticism that an offer supplies another religious
answer in place of the given prescription. It does not settle every question of
authenticity, meaning or application, or make an interpreter infallible. The same
standard applies to a Muslim adaptation and to this author’s argument. The
[Millati Islami case](ISLAMIC_APPLICATION.md#6-worked-case-an-islamic-adaptation-of-the-twelve-steps)
examines a published adaptation under that standard.

## 2. What continued drinking can establish

For specified circumstances, let **D** mean adequate application of specified spiritual
practices and **R** the promised relief, including the claim’s time scope. If `D → R`
is true, then `¬R → ¬D` follows by contraposition. Its application requires a justified
sufficiency premise and evidence of `¬R`. Continued drinking now does not by itself
establish failure of a promise concerning later relief. The outcome and time scope
must remain fixed.

If D means only abstaining, failure to abstain establishes its absence without
diagnosing other spiritual failings. If D combines several practices, the justified
premise and `¬R` establish only that the combined condition was not fulfilled, not
which element was lacking or that every practice was absent. A particular diagnosis
needs further evidence.

Defining adequate participation solely by a successful outcome prevents an unsuccessful
case from testing the assurance. Evidence of participation must therefore do more
than reclassify an outcome. These inferences concern what the programme promises;
they do not establish its religious authority.

## 3. Testimony, benefit and authority

Credible testimony and experience can contribute to religious knowledge without
providing deductive certainty. The [reply on experience](OBJECTIONS.md#6-can-experience-and-practice-supply-religious-knowledge)
preserves that point. Granting reported recoveries does not grant the programme’s
standing as a further religious answer. A benefit can be real without making the
associated spiritual course God’s prescription.

The [scope of the conclusion](THESIS.md#5-why-the-offer-does-not-deliver-the-prescribed-answer)
concerns what the offer delivers as religious direction. A person may learn elsewhere,
stop drinking and receive God’s assistance without that offer becoming the delivery
of the prescribed answer. Nor does the presence of a true teaching or a prescribed
act make the competing spiritual solution a completion the religion needed.

The authority and sufficiency of the Way of the Messengers are affirmative religious
commitments. Neither a gap in AA’s argument nor a failure of an assurance proves
them. Conversely, once that prescription is recognised as the sufficient religious
answer, another ungrounded spiritual solution has no remaining place to supply it.
